## Who is Semine?

[https://semine.com/en/](https://semine.com/en/)

Semine is an Accounts Payable automation platform provider with its own system, **DocFlow**, used by \~200–250 shared service centers and thousands of business units across multiple countries (Nordics plus others like Germany).

They focus on **accounts payable** documents: mainly invoices (PO / non-PO / contract-based) plus related credit memos and POs, often with large, complex line-item tables and attachments.

Today Semine uses **Abbyy Flexicapture** as the invoice capture engine, integrated with DocFlow. Flexicapture has processed \~4M invoices over 3–4 years, and they expect to continue processing very high volumes (hundreds of thousands to millions of invoices).

They are piloting **Docsumo as a replacement for Abbyy**: DocFlow will keep handling intake and orchestration, while Docsumo does extraction, validation and operator review. Key constraints:

* Multi-tenant setup (hundreds of shared service centers, thousands of business units/suppliers)

* Multi-country, multi-language invoices (Nordics, Germany, others) with country-specific validation logic

* Supplier- and customer-specific fields & rules

* Very large / matrix invoices and complex attachments

* Strict SLAs and desire to reduce manual operator time while maintaining or improving accuracy.

Volume \-\> Did 4 Million invoices via Abby in last 3 years

# Current Process (AS-IS) – Step-by-Step

## **Current Process (AS-IS) – Step-by-Step**

[https://drive.google.com/file/d/1WQI-Q\_8R880rIMpPTl\_QnY9BXZR\_tCvS/view?usp=sharing](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1WQI-Q_8R880rIMpPTl_QnY9BXZR_tCvS/view?usp=sharing)

### **1\. Invoice Intake into DocFlow**

* **Persona:** Supplier → *Customer AP team* → Semine/DocFlow

* **What happens:**

  * Suppliers send invoices (PDFs, sometimes multi-page) by email or other channels into Semine’s **DocFlow** platform.

  * DocFlow identifies whether a received file is an invoice and knows which **shared service center / customer organization** it belongs to.

* **Inputs:**

  * Email with PDF attachment(s)  
  * Some users used some portals too. 

  * DocFlow’s internal customer master (organization & client ID)

* **Outputs:**

  * Invoice record in DocFlow with:

    * Original PDF

    * `organization_id`, `client_id`, and a unique `document_id` generated by DocFlow.

### **2\. Sending Invoices from DocFlow to Abbyy Flexicapture**

* **Persona:** DocFlow system

* **What happens:**

  * For each invoice PDF deemed valid, DocFlow calls **Abbyy Flexicapture** via API.

  * The API request includes: organization ID, client ID, and document ID along with the PDF.

* **Inputs:**

  * Invoice PDF in DocFlow

  * Metadata: `organization_id`, `client_id`, `document_id`

* **Outputs:**

  * New document in Abbyy Flexicapture, inside the appropriate project, queued for extraction and validation.

### **3\. Supplier Identification in Abbyy**

* **Persona:** Flexicapture system \+ Semine admins

* **What happens:**

  * Semine uploads large **supplier tables** into Abbyy projects (one project ≈ one major configuration).

  * Due to Flexicapture limitations, they must **split suppliers across multiple projects** when the list becomes too large, and take frequent backups to avoid project corruption.

  * For each invoice, Abbyy tries to identify the supplier based on extracted:

    * Bank account / IBAN

    * VAT / organization number

    * Supplier name, etc.

* **Inputs:**

  * Invoice PDF

  * Supplier master lists (per project)

* **Outputs:**

  * Determined supplier (if matching works) or ambiguous supplier requiring manual operator resolution.

### **4\. Core Extraction: Header Fields & Line Items**

* **Persona:** Flexicapture system

* **What happens:**

  * Abbyy extracts:

    * Header fields (invoice number, invoice date, due date, totals, tax, payment reference, bank details, etc.)

    * Line-item tables (quantities, descriptions, amounts, VAT, etc.).

  * For multi-page invoices and attachments:

    * Abby extracts from all pages but accuracy is not good. 

    * If the sum of captured line-item net amounts does not equal the invoice net total, Abbyy flags this for operator intervention.

* **Inputs:**

  * Invoice PDF

* **Outputs:**

  * Extracted header and line-item data (provisional, pending validation and operator review).

### **5\. Validation & Country-Specific Rules**

* **Persona:** Flexicapture system \+ Semine configuration engineers

* **What happens:**

  * Abbyy applies a large set of **custom validation rules**, including:

    * Sum checks: line totals vs invoice totals

    * Algorithms for payment references (e.g., Norwegian **KID** and Danish **FIK** numbers) based on nationality

    * Country-specific mandatory fields and formatting (number formats, decimal separators, date formats).

  * They have built branching logic so that, for example, Norwegian invoices run Norwegian algorithms, Danish invoices run Danish ones, etc.

* **Inputs:**

  * Extracted fields from Abbyy

  * Country, supplier and customer metadata

* **Outputs:**

  * Validation result: pass/fail plus error reasons, sent to the operator queues.

### **6\. Operator Review & Correction (32+ Operators)**

* **Persona:**

  * **Junior operators** (primary reviewers)

  * **Senior operators** (escalations & complex cases)

  * A separate additional team (e.g., in Latvia) for some work.

* **What happens:**

  * Operators work **entirely inside Abbyy Flexicapture’s UI**.

  * Routing is relatively simple:

    1. DocFlow uses a first-in-first-out model; Flexicapture queues documents but no deeply advanced routing.

  * Process:

    1. Juniors pick invoices from their queue.

    2. They review fields flagged by validation or low confidence and correct them. Currently they review all documents. 

    3. If they cannot resolve issues (e.g., complex airline invoices, tricky validations), they escalate to seniors or specialist operators.

    4. Operators may manually extend tables if the invoice table spans additional pages but was not automatically captured.

* **Inputs:**

  * Abbyy UI view of the invoice \+ extracted data \+ validation errors.

* **Outputs:**

  * Corrected/approved documents ready for export back to DocFlow.

### **7\. Export Back to DocFlow & Downstream Posting**

* **Persona:** Flexicapture system → DocFlow → customer systems

* **What happens:**

  * Once an invoice is approved in Abbyy, extracted data is exported (e.g., as XML) back into DocFlow, keyed by the original `document_id`, `organization_id`, and `client_id`.

  * DocFlow uses this to:

    * Route to the correct client environment

    * Apply its own business logic (PO matching, credit memo handling, posting into the customer’s finance/ERP systems).

* **Inputs:**

  * Corrected extracted data from Abbyy

* **Outputs:**

  * Posted / ready-to-post invoice records in customer systems.

### **8\. Monitoring & Analytics**

* **Persona:** Semine product/ops team

* **What happens:**

  * They are building a platform to **benchmark accuracy and operator time** across different capture engines (Abbyy vs Docsumo vs others).

  * For now, they know:

    * Flexicapture has processed \~4M invoices over 3–4 years. Additional, they currently process around a million doc per year and expect it to increase to 2 million next year. 

    * Variable operator cost (time spent per invoice) is a major unknown they want to measure precisely for new providers.

# Tools, Manual Steps, and Pain Points

## **Tools, Manual Steps, and Pain Points**

### **Main Tools / Systems Today**

* **DocFlow** – Semine’s intake and orchestration platform (email intake, metadata, API to capture engines, routing to customer systems).

* **Abbyy Flexicapture** – Current OCR & invoice capture engine plus operator UI.

* **Supplier master / validation logic** – Large supplier lists and validation rules maintained per Abbyy project.

* **Internal benchmarking platform (in progress)** – To compare accuracy and operator time across providers.

### **Key Manual Steps & Bottlenecks**

1. **Administrative Overhead in Flexicapture**

   * Need to **split supplier lists across multiple projects** because Flexicapture is optimized for single-customer scenarios; Semine has 200+ customers and thousands of business units.

   * Must take **regular backups** and occasionally restore from backups due to risk of project corruption in a legacy Windows application.

   * Result: High admin workload and configuration fragmentation; hard to roll out improvements uniformly.

2. **Complex Validation Logic per Country & Supplier**

   * Validations (KID/FIK algorithms, country-specific mandatory fields, numeric formatting rules) are implemented in Flexicapture with extensive custom code and branching.

   * Hard to **reuse and maintain** across many projects and customers.

   * Risk of inconsistent behavior between projects if rules diverge over time.

3. **Manual Handling of Multi-Page / Matrix Invoices**

   * Current heuristic: capture the first table, treat remaining pages as attachments. If line-item sums don’t match totals, operators must manually capture additional tables/pages.

   * Matrix/retail invoices with very large tables are common and painful; they require more operator time and are a key target for improvement.

4. **Operator Queue Management and Specialization**

   * Routing is mostly **first-in-first-out**; not very intelligent.

   * Some operators specialize in specific countries or complex suppliers (e.g., airlines), which is managed informally rather than through structured routing rules.

   * 32+ operators (juniors and seniors) means large variable cost; they need better visibility into average handling time per invoice and ways to reduce it.

5. **Supplier Identification with Mixed Data Quality**

   * When invoices and supplier lists have high-quality data (IBAN, VAT numbers), matching works well; when only names exist or are inconsistently formatted, matching becomes harder and may need manual work.

   * Currently largely handled via Abbyy \+ Semine logic, with manual interventions when needed.

6. **Handling Very Large Invoices / Files**

   * They see extreme cases: invoices with hundreds or even tens of thousands of pages (some misconfigured telecom or utility bills).

   * Large numbers of invoice lines can overwhelm downstream systems, so they need mechanisms to **limit pages and/or split intelligently**. Today this is handled via rules and operator workarounds.

7. **Lack of Transparent Confidence / Guidance for Reviewers**

   * In Flexicapture and early Docsumo tests, low confidence is mostly represented as a color (yellow/orange) without clear textual guidance.

   * They want tooltips that clearly tell operators and eventual self-service users:

     * *What’s wrong?*

     * *What should I do about it?*

### **Risks & Impact**

* **Errors:** Mis-extracted totals, payment references, or supplier data can lead to wrong postings; hence the heavy reliance on validation logic.

* **Delays / SLA impact:** Any file that goes into error or requires manual rework can affect SLAs for Semine’s customers; they treat each failed file as high impact.

* **Cost:** Operator time is the main variable cost; admin overhead in Flexicapture adds hidden cost and limits scalability.

# Notes

## **. What are the Norwegian & Danish algorithmic checks?**

On the 31 July call, Jarl breaks down how they use **different validation algorithms** in Flexicapture based on the *nationality* of the invoice. Specifically:

* For **Norway** they have a payment reference called **KID**.

* For **Denmark** they have a payment reference called **FIK**.

* Both are **payment identification numbers** – “a long number” printed on the invoice, used by banks/ERPs to match incoming payments to invoices.

In Abbyy Flexicapture, Semine has:

* One or more **fields on the invoice** that hold this payment reference (KID or FIK).

* For those fields, they’ve implemented **algorithms** that:

  * Check the **structure/format** of the number (length, allowed characters, etc.).

  * Run **validation logic** (think checksum-style rules) so they can say:  
     “Is this payment reference *valid* according to the Norwegian KID rules or the Danish FIK rules?”

### **How they decide which algorithm to run**

They describe two possible patterns:

1. **Branch by nationality (preferred design)**

   * If invoice nationality is **Norwegian** → run **KID algorithm** on the reference field.

   * If invoice nationality is **Danish** → run **FIK algorithm**.

   * They also expect different *other* fields to be present depending on nationality (e.g., “we should expect some particular fields for Norwegian invoices and other fields for Danish invoices”).

2. **Run all algorithms and accept if any passes (fallback design)**

   * They explicitly say: you could just run *all* algorithms on the same number and “if at least one of them passes, then OK, we approve the number.”

   * That way they’re **not completely dependent** on correctly classifying the nationality in every case.

So conceptually, their Flexicapture logic looks something like:

`if nationality == "Norwegian":`  
    `valid = check_KID(reference_number)`  
`elif nationality == "Danish":`  
    `valid = check_FIK(reference_number)`  
`else:`  
    `valid = check_KID(reference_number) OR check_FIK(reference_number)`

`if not valid:`  
    `send_to_operator_queue_with_error("Invalid payment reference")`

⚠️ Important: They never describe the *internal math* of KID/FIK (weights, mod, etc.) on the call – only that they “run algorithms” for those numbers. Any deeper detail beyond that would be outside the transcript.

## **2\. How Abbyy projects are used by Semine**

Jarl also spends time explaining **how they structure Abbyy projects** and why it’s painful for their scale.

### **2.1. What is an Abbyy “project” for them?**

* In Flexicapture terminology, a **project** is basically:

  * The entire configuration for one “solution”: document definitions, fields, rules, supplier lists, etc.

  * Jarl says it’s roughly equivalent to one of our **document types \+ config** in Docsumo.

So instead of one single global setup, they have **multiple projects**.

### **2.2. Supplier tables inside projects**

For supplier identification, they:

1. **Upload a giant table of all known suppliers** into a project.

2. Flexicapture then uses that table to match suppliers based on:

   * Supplier name

   * Bank account / IBAN

   * VAT / org number, etc. (they mention these in later calls on supplier matching, but the key idea is: supplier table is central).

Because Semine has:

* \~200 customers

* Several thousand business units / suppliers

…a single project can’t hold everything comfortably.

### **2.3. Why they need multiple projects**

They describe two major Flexicapture issues:

1. **Risk of project corruption (old Windows app)**

   * Flexicapture is a local Windows application “from the early 2000s”; projects can be corrupted or broken.

   * Because of that, they must **take regular backups** and sometimes **restore from backup**.

2. **Scaling limits on suppliers per project**

   * They can “only have a certain number of suppliers per project.”

   * After that, they have to **duplicate the project** and **split the supplier list** between them.

   * Result: they now manage **two (or more) separate projects** which both need to be kept up to date.

Example from the transcript:  
 they upload a giant supplier table into one project, hit practical limits, then **copy the entire project** and split supplier lists across project A and project B. Any change to validation logic, fields, etc. has to be repeated in both.

### **2.4. How validations (incl. KID/FIK) sit inside these projects**

Within each **project**, they have:

* All the **validation logic**:

  * Field comparisons (e.g., sums of lines vs totals).

  * Country-specific checks: “we need to compare several different fields” and “check some algorithms on some other fields.”

  * The **branching logic** for Norwegian vs Danish invoices (KID vs FIK) and potentially other nationalities.

Because validations live **inside each project**, and they have multiple projects due to supplier scaling & corruption risk:

* They end up with **duplicated validation logic** scattered across projects.

* If they improve a KID check in Project A, they also have to remember to update Project B, C, etc.

* Jarl calls these “workarounds” that make it **hard to apply improvements across all clients and suppliers**.

## **3\. How to think about it for Docsumo**

Grounded in what they said, but phrased as product guidance for you:

### **What Semine wants to keep (behavior-wise)**

From their perspective, **what must be preserved** when moving from Abbyy → Docsumo is:

1. **KID/FIK-type algorithmic validations** still exist:

   * Ability to run country-specific checks on payment reference fields.

   * Ability to run several algorithms on the same number and accept if “at least one passes”.

2. **Branching logic based on nationality**:

   * Norwegian vs Danish vs others – each can have different required fields \+ algorithms.

3. **Centralized, reusable configuration** (this part is strongly implied):

   * They explicitly call out the pain of having to copy rules into many Flexicapture projects.

   * What they want from Docsumo is effectively **one place to maintain these validations**, then apply them across customers.

### **A concrete example from the call**

Rephrasing their example in Docsumo-terms (logic taken directly from how they described Abbyy):

* Suppose an invoice comes in with:

  1. Nationality: **Norwegian**

  2. Payment reference field: `1234567890…` (long number)

* Then the pipeline should:

  1. Detect nationality \= `NO`.

  2. Run **KID algorithm** on that reference:

     * If it passes → mark field as valid, no error.

     * If it fails → either:

       * Also try **FIK algorithm** (as backup), and if that passes, approve; or

       * Raise validation error: “Invalid Norwegian payment reference (KID) – please check.”

  3. Only if *no* algorithm passes should it be sent to operators.
